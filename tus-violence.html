Media Attention and Specialty Choice: Evidence from Healthcare Violence Coverage in Türkiye

Role Solo — design, data, analysis
Coverage 40 branches · 26 exam periods · 2013–2025
Methods NLP · LLM annotation · Panel OLS · Spearman
Tools Python · pdfplumber · statsmodels · linearmodels · Claude Haiku

Overview

Tests whether media intensity around healthcare violence in Türkiye correlates with revealed shifts in medical specialty preferences, measured through branch-level minimum scores on the Turkish Medical Specialization Examination (TUS) from 2013 to 2025. Branches are classified by exposure to patient contact (high / intermediate / low) following the Dorsey et al. (2003) controllable lifestyle framework.

The central finding is group-specific: media intensity is negatively associated with minimum scores in high- and intermediate-exposure branches, while low-exposure branches show no such pattern — consistent with a violence-specific deterrence signal rather than a general decline in TUS competitiveness.

Data

  • Outcome: branch-level minimum TUS scores across 40 specialty branches × 26 exam periods (2013–2025); 46,808 branch-period observations after filtering. Extracted from official ÖSYM PDF publications using a pdfplumber-based parser.
  • Treatment D1 (media intensity): annual Google News article counts on five Turkish-language healthcare violence keywords, validated against official Beyaz Kod (White Code) incident notifications (Spearman r = 0.786, p = 0.036 over seven overlapping years).
  • Treatment D2 (discourse intensity): annual proportion of violence-flagged entries from a purposively constructed Ekşi Sözlük corpus (4,148 entries across 54 threads).

Methods

  • Multi-stage annotation pipeline: failed unsupervised topic modeling (LDA, BERTopic) → keyword scoring → two-annotator human labeling on 200 entries (Cohen's κ = 0.59 for physical violence) → few-shot LLM extension to the remaining 3,948 entries using Claude Haiku, validated against the human-labeled subset (κ = 0.54 against union ground truth).
  • Spearman rank correlations between each treatment and the group-year mean of minimum scores.
  • Year-level OLS with exposure-group interaction terms, controlling for time trends in two specifications (quadratic polynomial and year fixed effects).
  • Branch-level panel OLS with branch fixed effects, log(quota) control, and entity-clustered standard errors (46,808 observations).
  • Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis across all 13 years to verify that no single year drives the interaction findings.

What This Project Demonstrates

An end-to-end text-as-data pipeline with explicit annotation quality reporting, applied to a substantively meaningful labor-economics question. Combines NLP (annotation, LLM extension, validation), econometric design (interaction terms, panel fixed effects, sensitivity analysis), and a careful framing that does not overstate what the data can identify.
